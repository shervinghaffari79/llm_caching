\subsection{Admission Baselines}\label{subsec:admission_baselines}
ReuseGate is compared with replacement and admission policies on the cache of Section~\ref{subsec:baseline_comparison}, with the same bi-encoder, exact FAISS index, streams, answers and batch size, so that the policies differ only in the decision about which entries stay. Open admission inserts every miss; LRU, FIFO, ARC, S3-FIFO, SIEVE, GDSF and DynamicAdaptiveClimb are reimplemented from their published descriptions, with the resizing of DynamicAdaptiveClimb disabled so that every policy holds the same number of entries. W-TinyLFU admits the entry that leaves a small window only if its count (exact in this paper) exceeds that of the victim of the main segment.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SOLAR} \citep{sun2026solar} stores an entry when the accumulated regret of the requests exceeds an adaptive threshold, and chooses victims by Thompson sampling over per-entry Beta posteriors. SOLAR-A uses the admission gate with a recency, frequency and diversity eviction score, and SOLAR-E uses Thompson-sampling eviction with open admission. The authors' agent computes regret from a graded feedback score of the answer; a response cache has no such score, so the regret of a request is 1 for a miss and 1 minus the cosine similarity of the served entry for a hit. All other constants are those of the authors' code (public repository, Apache-2.0 licence, commit 8d01e93), and the implementation follows its control flow call for call. Run beside the authors' unmodified agents on the same requests, it serves, inserts and evicts identically in every one of 5,000 requests in each of two search-log windows, for SOLAR and SOLAR-A at capacities 250 and 1,000 and for SOLAR-E at 250, and in 3,000 requests of SOLAR-E at 1,000.
\item \textbf{ReuseGate} (Section~\ref{subsubsec:learned_admission}) and its labeled variant \textbf{ReuseGate-R}.
\end{itemize}
The policies choose their own admission rate, which Table~\ref{tab:admission_matched} reports as the share of misses that are inserted once the cache is full. Every threshold is selected on tuning windows that precede the evaluation windows (Section~\ref{subsubsec:leakage_controls}). ReuseGate reads reference traffic of 50,000 requests on the search log and 21,265 on LmArena; SOLAR learns online, and its gate starts empty in every window. SOLAR is also run unmodified on LoCoMo, the setting of its authors, where ReuseGate is applied as a feedback-free demand rule (Section~\ref{subsec:admission_baselines_results}).
\\
Against its closest prior art, ReuseGate differs from SOLAR in the signal and the learner: SOLAR gates admission by the accumulated regret of served answers, whereas ReuseGate predicts from features available at arrival whether a later request falls within the serving threshold, with a supervised classifier trained on reuse outcomes that the request log of the same workload reveals.

\subsection{ReuseGate against SOLAR and Further Replacement Policies}
\label{subsec:admission_baselines_results}
Table~\ref{tab:admission_matched} reports, for each policy at the threshold chosen for $\lambda=1$, the share of misses it inserts once the cache is full, the correct-hit ratio, the false-hit rate, the precision of served hits and the break-even price $\lambda^{*}$, the false-hit price at which the policy's score falls to zero. The policies admit very different shares. Open admission, FIFO and SOLAR-E insert every miss, SOLAR and SOLAR-A insert [[SL admission share]] of them on the search log and 0.22 on LmArena, ReuseGate 0.41 on LmArena [[SL]] and ReuseGate-R 0.35 [[SL]].
\\
On LmArena, ReuseGate serves 19.49\% of requests correctly against 15.81\% for SOLAR, 15.55\% for SOLAR-A and 15.53\% for SOLAR-E, at the same precision of served hits (82.4\% against 82.2--82.8\%). Its score exceeds that of SOLAR-A by 3.94, 3.05 and 2.16 points at $\lambda=0$, 1 and 2 (nine windows, Holm $p<0.001$); at $\lambda=5$ the difference is $-0.21$ points (95\% interval $-0.70$ to $+0.28$, Holm $p=0.40$), and at $\lambda=10$ SOLAR-A is ahead by 2.76 points (Holm $p<0.001$). Against SOLAR the differences are $+3.69$, $+2.82$, $+1.95$, $-0.50$ ($p=0.22$) and $-3.02$, and against SOLAR-E $+3.96$, $+3.16$, $+2.37$, $-0.32$ ($p=0.40$) and $-2.46$. ReuseGate-R, which prices false hits with earlier labels, exceeds SOLAR, SOLAR-A and SOLAR-E at every $\lambda$, by 3.96 to 21.76 points (Holm $p<0.001$). SOLAR was also run in the setting it was built for. The authors' code, unmodified, manages an experience buffer of $K=10$ entries for an agent on LoCoMo (ten sessions, three seeds, 30 session-seed runs per policy, token F1 of the held-out questions, the authors' like and dislike feedback), with Qwen3-4B as the agent model. FIFO reaches an F1 of 0.249. SOLAR, SOLAR-A and SOLAR-E reach 0.278, 0.269 and 0.267, which exceeds FIFO by 0.029 (95\% interval 0.014 to 0.043), 0.020 (0.010 to 0.030) and 0.019 (0.005 to 0.032), paired over runs; LRU, ARC and LFU do not differ from FIFO. ReuseGate (demand rule), a score that counts earlier questions within a cosine radius of 0.6 of an entry's question and admits a newcomer only if its count exceeds the lowest resident score, uses neither feedback nor labels and reaches 0.261, which differs from FIFO by $+0.012$ ($-0.001$ to $+0.026$) and from SOLAR by $-0.016$ ($-0.030$ to $-0.003$); it does not differ from SOLAR-A ($-0.008$, $-0.021$ to $+0.005$) or SOLAR-E ($-0.006$, $-0.020$ to $+0.008$). A learned ReuseGate cannot be fitted there, since each session offers about 160 warm-up examples.
\\
On the four LLM-free workloads shipped with the SOLAR repository (authors' code unmodified; three seeds, and ten seeds for the comparisons with FIFO and SOLAR-A), ReuseGate (demand rule) keeps a stable subset when the cache is smaller than a cyclic scan: with a scan of 20 items and 10 slots it reaches a hit rate of 0.45 against 0.40 for SOLAR-A and SOLAR and 0.08 for LRU. It trails SOLAR-A on small caches with uniform popularity, by 0.08 to 0.13 at 10 to 20 slots with the radius of 0.8 selected on separate tuning seeds, and it trails FIFO and SOLAR-A when the topic changes every 100 steps. Table~\ref{tab:solar_synthetic} in the appendix lists the hit rates of all policies.
