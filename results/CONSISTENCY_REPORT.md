## 1. The latency / response-time contradiction

With the published full-stacking overhead of 539.80 ms, the average response time cannot fall below **0.540 s** regardless of hit ratio, because the similarity check runs on every request before the outcome is known. The published average of 0.311 s is below that floor, and below the 520.76 ms cross-encoder term alone.

At the measured generation latency of 0.854 s, the expected averages are:

- full stacking: **0.597 s**
- bi-encoder only: **0.071 s**

Only the second is compatible with a sub-second average, which identifies which configuration the response-time experiment must have used.

## 4. Normalisation and fold construction

For every result in this file, z-score normalisation is applied by a StandardScaler that is the first step of an sklearn Pipeline. The Pipeline is instantiated and fitted inside the fold loop on the training indices only, then used to transform the validation indices. Statistics from validation folds therefore never enter training.

Measured effect of the incorrect protocol (scaler fitted before splitting): dF1 = +0.00060, dAUC = -0.00009.

Fold construction matters more: under plain StratifiedKFold 27.9% of validation questions have already been seen in training via another pair, versus 0.0% under StratifiedGroupKFold over question components. Leakage-driven F1 inflation: **+0.0373**.

Row-level duplication in QQP: 559 exact repeats, 250 mirrored repeats, 869 self-pairs, 35 label conflicts; all removed before sampling (363846 -> 362133 rows).

## 7. Eviction policy and weight sensitivity

Across 21 weight settings on the simplex, hit ratio spans 2.8 percentage points (std 0.57). 95% of settings still beat LRU at capacity 500. The paper's weights (0.28/0.38/0.34) give 61.6%, best on the grid is 61.8%.


## 8. Does the ablation support the title?

Cheap, bi-encoder-derived features add +0.0163 F1 over the bare cosine for 2.18 ms per request. The cross-encoder adds +0.0045 F1 for 60.1 ms. Its 95% bootstrap CI includes zero, so on this data its contribution is not distinguishable from noise. The title's claim is supported on a per-millisecond basis and should be defended in those terms in the text, not left to the title alone.

| Component | Class | dF1 | Added latency | dF1 per 100 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Element-wise ops (product, |diff|) | cheap | +0.0231 | 0.010 ms | +220.6320 |
| Distance metrics (L2, L1) | cheap | -0.0063 | 0.021 ms | -29.9280 |
| Text features | cheap | +0.0223 | 0.027 ms | +83.8139 |
| LightGBM meta-classifier inference | cheap | +nan | 2.107 ms | - |
| Cross-encoder (stsb-roberta-large) | expensive | +0.0045 | 60.128 ms | +0.0074 |

**Definitions.** Cheap: arithmetic over bi-encoder vectors already computed by the retrieval step; marginal cost is a vector operation. Not encoder-free: they consume bi-encoder output. Expensive: a second forward pass with quadratic attention over the concatenated pair, per candidate, unable to reuse either side's cached embedding.


**Why keep the cross-encoder at all?** The measurements say it can be dropped for latency-sensitive deployments. Reasons to keep it as an optional stage, which should be argued explicitly rather than left implicit: (1) it is the only component that models token-level interaction between the two queries, so it degrades differently from the embedding-derived features under distribution shift; (2) on the STS regression formulation the ensemble uses its continuous score, where a threshold-free signal matters more than in binary cache decisions; (3) in an offline batching regime its amortised cost falls by an order of magnitude, so the trade-off differs for batch re-validation of a cache versus online serving. None of these justify keeping it on the online path by default, and the paper should recommend the cross-encoder-free configuration as the deployment default.
